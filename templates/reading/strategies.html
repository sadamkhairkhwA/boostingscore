Golden rule

Answer from the passage, not background knowledge. If it is not written (or clearly implied), do not invent it — especially for Not Given.

Skim first

Read headings and the first line of each paragraph before you touch the questions — build a map of the text in under two minutes.

Read these only when skimming

Solar energy has transformed how cities manage power. Critics point to storage limitations as the key unresolved obstacle. Battery research now focuses on density and cycle life rather than headline cost per panel.

Highlighted = what you read. You know the topic without reading every word.

Keywords

Underline key terms in the question, then scan the passage for synonyms — IELTS rarely repeats the same wording.

Question

What did scientists discover about ocean temperatures?

Passage (synonyms)

Researchers found that sea surface heat had risen significantly.

scientists → Researchers · discover → found · ocean temperatures → sea surface heat

Paragraph mapping

Label each paragraph with a three-word gist before matching headings or information — you answer from the map, not from memory.

Example map
Para A: History of fossil fuels from the industrial era…History of energy
Para B: Renewable infrastructure barriers in grid design…Current challenges
Para C: Battery technology projections through 2040…Future solutions

TFNG discipline

If the passage does not mention the claim, choose Not Given — not False. False needs a direct contradiction.

Statement

Scientists have proven that coffee improves long-term memory.

Passage

Some studies suggest a possible link between caffeine and short-term alertness.

NOT GIVEN

The passage hedges (“suggest”, “possible”) and never “proves” memory — but it also never denies it outright, so it is not False.

Time boxing

Aim for roughly twenty minutes per passage so Part 3 still gets full attention.

60-minute visual split
Part 1 · 20 min
Part 2 · 20 min
Part 3 · 20 min

≈13–14 questions per part · ~90 seconds per question on average · move on if stuck.

Gap-fill grammar

The gap must be grammatically correct in the sentence and match the passage meaning — check word class before you copy.

The scheme led to a measurable drop in during morning peaks.

produce ✗ noun/verb clash congestion ✓ fits + matches passage vehicles ✗ plausible but wrong meaning

{# ——— Tab 3: Question guides ——— #}

Question guides — worked examples

True / False / Not Given

Is the statement supported, contradicted, or absent from the passage?

Passage

The Amazon produces 20% of the world’s oxygen. Deforestation has accelerated since 2015. No international agreement limits logging.

True “Amazon produces 20% of oxygen” — directly stated.
False “Deforestation has slowed” — passage says accelerated (opposite).
Not Given “Brazil supports an agreement” — not mentioned at all.

Yes / No / Not Given

About the writer’s opinion — look for argues, believes, suggests, insists.

Passage

The author suggests regulations could help reduce emissions.

Statement: The author believes stricter laws are necessary.

Not Given suggests / could ≠ believes / necessary — opinion is hedged, not stated as belief.

Matching headings

Match the main idea of the whole paragraph — not one detail.

Paragraph

Para 1 Installation costs for household solar have fallen faster than policy analysts predicted, yet uptake remains uneven across regions because financing rules still favour large commercial buyers.

  • ii. The history of solar panel inventionToo narrow — invention isn’t the focus.
  • iv. Commercial buyers and tax creditsTrue detail inside the paragraph, but not the whole main idea.
  • ✓ vii. Falling costs versus uneven household uptake — matches cost drop + regional uptake gap.

Gap-fill

Copy exactly from the passage when instructions require passage words.

The report attributes the delay chiefly to in supply chains.

Passage excerpt

…the delay chiefly to disruption in supply chains…

Copy exactly — do not paraphrase. Answer: disruption (same spelling as the passage).

Skills lab — interactive exercises

Scanning = moving your eyes quickly to find one specific detail. Don’t read every word.

The council introduced new parking rules near the station to reduce congestion during peak hours. Changes took effect in March 2022, following months of consultation. Officials said the scheme had cut traffic by 18%.

When did the new rules take effect? Tap the answer in the passage.

Skim the paragraph while the bar runs down (~8 seconds), then answer from memory of the main idea only.

Urban forests cool neighbourhoods, absorb stormwater, and improve mental health. Cities from Singapore to Medellín now treat green corridors as core infrastructure rather than optional parks.

What is the paragraph mainly about?

Pick the option that keeps the same meaning as the original sentence.

Original: The university decided to reduce expenditure across departments.

Short passage — choose True, False, or Not Given for each statement, then submit.

Remote teams often report stable productivity after the first year. Managers rarely cite video fatigue as the main obstacle; instead, unclear priorities dominate exit surveys.

1. Video fatigue is the primary reason managers struggle with remote teams.

2. Productivity among remote teams usually collapses after the first year.

3. Unclear priorities feature prominently in exit surveys.

Opinion items — Yes / No / Not Given. Hedged language often means Not Given.

The author acknowledges that stricter rent controls might calm prices temporarily, but questions whether they discourage new building.

1. The author supports rent controls without reservation.

2. The author has personally invested in rental property.

3. The author suggests rent controls could have a short-term price effect.

Read at a natural exam pace, tap Done when finished, then answer from memory. Your rough speed is estimated from word count ÷ time.

The regional rail authority announced a phased upgrade of signalling equipment across three lines over the next eighteen months. Peak-hour delays are expected during weekends while contractors replace outdated cables. Passengers are advised to allow an extra fifteen minutes for connections until work completes in December. Officials claim modern signalling could cut average wait times by almost a quarter once systems stabilise. Critics argue communications around disruptions remain vague, and that smaller stations still lack real-time boards.

1. What is mainly being upgraded?

2. When is the work expected to complete?

60-minute time plan

Visual split
Part 1
0–20 min
Part 2
20–40 min
Part 3
40–60 min

13–14 questions per part · ~90 seconds per question on average · move on if stuck.

Part 1 — 0 to 20 min

Easiest passage. Focus on accuracy, not speed.

Key tactic
Skim the paragraph openings, then tackle questions in order — answers usually follow the passage.

Part 2 — 20 to 40 min

Medium difficulty. Often contains matching headings or Y/N/NG.

Key tactic
Map each paragraph’s main idea before matching — don’t reread the whole passage for every question.

Part 3 — 40 to 60 min

Hardest passage — stamina and inference load are highest.

Key tactic
Flag a letter for any item over 90 seconds, move on, and return only if time remains after Part 3 is complete.

Common time traps

Spending 8+ minutes on one question

Fix: mark and move on — you can miss two hard items and still band well if the rest are solid.

Re-reading the full passage for every question

Fix: use your paragraph map and keywords; return to the passage only for proof.

Leaving Part 3 with only 10 minutes

Fix: set a mental alarm at 20 minutes into Part 2 to enter Part 3 on time.

Forgetting to transfer answers

Fix: keep the last 2 minutes on the clock strictly for transfer and spelling checks.
