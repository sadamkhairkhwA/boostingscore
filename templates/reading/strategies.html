{# ——— Stage 3: Skills lab ——— #}

Skills lab — interactive drills

Short exercises to sharpen scanning, skimming, paraphrase recognition, and exam pace.

Scanning = moving your eyes quickly to find one specific detail. Don't read every word.

The council introduced new parking rules near the station to reduce congestion during peak hours. Changes took effect in March 2022, following months of consultation. Officials said the scheme had cut traffic by 18%.

When did the new rules take effect? Tap the answer in the passage.

Skim the paragraph while the bar runs down (~8 seconds), then answer from memory of the main idea only.

Urban forests cool neighbourhoods, absorb stormwater, and improve mental health. Cities from Singapore to Medellín now treat green corridors as core infrastructure rather than optional parks.

What is the paragraph mainly about?

Pick the option that keeps the same meaning as the original sentence.

Original: The university decided to reduce expenditure across departments.

Short passage — choose True, False, or Not Given for each statement, then submit.

Remote teams often report stable productivity after the first year. Managers rarely cite video fatigue as the main obstacle; instead, unclear priorities dominate exit surveys.

1. Video fatigue is the primary reason managers struggle with remote teams.

2. Productivity among remote teams usually collapses after the first year.

3. Unclear priorities feature prominently in exit surveys.

Opinion items — Yes / No / Not Given. Hedged language often means Not Given.

The author acknowledges that stricter rent controls might calm prices temporarily, but questions whether they discourage new building.

1. The author supports rent controls without reservation.

2. The author has personally invested in rental property.

3. The author suggests rent controls could have a short-term price effect.

Read at a natural exam pace, tap Done when finished, then answer from memory. Your rough speed is estimated from word count ÷ time.

The regional rail authority announced a phased upgrade of signalling equipment across three lines over the next eighteen months. Peak-hour delays are expected during weekends while contractors replace outdated cables. Passengers are advised to allow an extra fifteen minutes for connections until work completes in December. Officials claim modern signalling could cut average wait times by almost a quarter once systems stabilise. Critics argue communications around disruptions remain vague, and that smaller stations still lack real-time boards.

1. What is mainly being upgraded?

2. When is the work expected to complete?

{# ——— Stage 4: Time plan ——— #}

60-minute time plan

Visual split
Part 1
0–20 min
Part 2
20–40 min
Part 3
40–60 min

13–14 questions per part · ~90 seconds per question on average · move on if stuck.

Part 1 — 0 to 20 min

Easiest passage. Focus on accuracy, not speed.

Key tactic
Skim the paragraph openings, then tackle questions in order — answers usually follow the passage.

Part 2 — 20 to 40 min

Medium difficulty. Often contains matching headings or Y/N/NG.

Key tactic
Map each paragraph's main idea before matching — don't reread the whole passage for every question.

Part 3 — 40 to 60 min

Hardest passage — stamina and inference load are highest.

Key tactic
Flag a letter for any item over 90 seconds, move on, and return only if time remains after Part 3 is complete.

Common time traps

Spending 8+ minutes on one question

Fix: mark and move on — you can miss two hard items and still band well if the rest are solid.

Re-reading the full passage for every question

Fix: use your paragraph map and keywords; return to the passage only for proof.

Leaving Part 3 with only 10 minutes

Fix: set a mental alarm at 20 minutes into Part 2 to enter Part 3 on time.

Forgetting to transfer answers

Fix: keep the last 2 minutes on the clock strictly for transfer and spelling checks.
