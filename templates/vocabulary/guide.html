How to use this website

{% bs_icon "folder" "green" "md" %}

Flashcards

The core tool. See a word, try to recall the meaning yourself, then flip. Rate Hard if you struggled, Easy if you knew it. Hard cards come back again in the same session. Do this daily for 10–15 minutes.

{% bs_icon "clock" "green" "md" %}

Smart review

Words you marked Hard are scheduled to come back. The system tracks when each word is due. Always do your due reviews before starting new words.

{% bs_icon "quiz" "purple" "md" %}

Quick quiz

Tests your knowledge under pressure. Use Multiple choice first when a word is new. Switch to Spell it and Fill in the blank once you’re more confident — those force active recall which is far more effective.

{% bs_icon "pen" "purple" "md" %}

Type it

Write a full sentence using the word. This is the most powerful exercise for Writing and Speaking band improvement. Don’t skip it.

{% bs_icon "clipboard" "navy" "md" %}

Word list

Browse all words by topic and level. Star words you want to revisit. Use this to plan which topic to study next before your exam.

{% bs_icon "book" "green" "md" %}

Custom decks

Build your own decks from words you encounter in practice tests or reading. Personalised vocabulary sticks better than generic lists.

Why vocabulary matters in IELTS

Vocabulary accounts for 25% of your Writing and Speaking score directly, and affects Reading and Listening comprehension too.

  • Examiners look for range (variety of words), accuracy (correct meaning and form), and collocation — natural word combinations like “raise awareness” not “increase awareness”.
  • Don’t just learn definitions — learn the word in a phrase. “Mitigate the impact” is more useful than knowing “mitigate” alone.
  • Band 6 uses basic vocabulary correctly. Band 7 uses less common words accurately. Band 8+ uses sophisticated vocabulary naturally with collocations.
  • Passive vocabulary (words you recognise) is not enough. You need active vocabulary — words you can use spontaneously in writing and speech.
  • Topic-specific vocabulary is high-yield. Environment, Health, Technology, Education, and Society appear in most Writing Task 2 questions. Master these first.
  • Aim to learn 10 new words per day with context, not just a list. Review them the next day, then again in 3 days, then in a week.

What vocabulary gets you each band

Band 5

Limited range, repetition of basic words, errors in word choice. Relies on simple vocabulary from everyday topics.

Band 6

Adequate range for familiar topics, some less common words attempted but with errors. Meaning is clear but choices feel generic.

Band 7

Uses less common and topic-specific vocabulary with generally good awareness. Some collocations used naturally. Minor inaccuracies only.

Band 8

Wide range used fluently and flexibly. Strong collocations, idiomatic awareness, rare errors. Words chosen feel precise and natural throughout.

Band 9

Full command. Wide, sophisticated, and accurate vocabulary used effortlessly. Collocations, register, and nuance all handled correctly.

To move from Band 6 to 7, focus on replacing overused words (big → substantial, show → demonstrate, important → crucial) and learning topic collocations.

A simple weekly vocabulary plan

Monday

Learn 10 new words from your target topic using Flashcards. Don’t rush. Focus on meaning + example sentence.

Tuesday

Do Smart review (due words). Then use Quick quiz (Multiple choice) on Monday’s words.

Wednesday

Type it: write one sentence for each word you struggled with. Check your sentences.

Thursday

Learn 10 more new words. Do due reviews first.

Friday

Quiz yourself with Fill in the blank and Spell it on all words from this week.

Saturday

Free review: browse Word list, star anything you’re unsure about, redo Hard flashcards.

Sunday

Rest or light review only. Consistency over intensity.

Three focused weeks on one topic — 60–90 words with full context — will have a measurable impact on your writing band score.
